## The claim

**Self-supervised pretraining on a 9,480-spectrum public NMR corpus does not
beat standard machine learning on small clinical cohorts. Neither more spectra,
a more even study mix, more parameters, nor synthetic spectra closes the gap
within the range public data allows.**

All three gates of the PI's decision tree were reached and answered. The project
has a terminal result.

## The three results, in the order they should be written

### 1. Standard ML wins or ties everywhere, and it is not an artefact

Table 2. Every comparison is paired on identical splits, with the same readout
for both arms (scaled features + balanced logistic regression). Few-shot budgets
are exact (5, 10, 20 or 40 patients per group, dropped if any group would keep
fewer than 5 for testing); full data uses LOOCV for n < 100 and 10x5-fold CV
otherwise; intervals are paired 95% CIs (episodes) or a paired bootstrap (LOOCV).

| pretraining | ours better | standard ML better | level | mean difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| masking, mean pool | 0 | 17 | 4 | -0.067 |
| masking, 16-region pool | 0 | 17 | 4 | -0.048 |
| jigsaw | 0 | 19 | 2 | -0.124 |
| joint | 0 | 19 | 2 | -0.108 |

The four level cells for masking are all full-data cells with small n (BrC-T2D
cancer, diabetes and 4-class under LOOCV, and Barth). The closest is BrC-T2D
cancer: +0.012 [-0.030, +0.053] with 16-region pooling. The correct statement is
**"standard ML is better or statistically level everywhere, and the pretrained
model is never better."**

The result is *not* explained by:
- **a broken corpus**: it holds on the rebuilt, verified corpus (lactate CH3 at
  1.325 against a true 1.330, span 0.117 ppm).
- **the readout**: averaging the 128 tokens discards chemical-shift position;
  keeping it (16-region pooling) narrows the gap by 0.019 but changes no cell's
  verdict.
- **freezing the backbone**: fine-tuning the last block changes the frozen probe
  by -0.008 and full fine-tuning by -0.000 (Table 3). **[revised]** The
  2026-09-25 version said deeper adaptation was *worse* (-0.010); with the
  corrected labels it is level, so "fine-tuning does not help", not "it hurts".
- **a useless pretext task**: masking beats the same network untrained by +0.046.
- **model size**: Table 5 and the Phase 6 section below.

### 2. More of the same data does not close the gap  **[revised]**

Table 4. 45 pretraining runs over three axes.

- **Spectra.** At constant study mix (effective 4.1 studies), going from 858 to
  6,410 spectra gains +0.003 per doubling at full data (p = 0.17, permutation)
  and +0.004 at 10 per group (p = 0.13). Not significant.
- **Study mix.** At fixed budgets of 2,000 and 4,000 spectra, effective studies
  is not a significant predictor (p = 0.19 at full data, 0.25 at 10 per group).
  In the pooled regression over all 45 runs it just passes at full data
  (p = 0.043) and fails at 10 per group (p = 0.14); spectrum count is significant
  there (p = 0.003) but small (under 0.01 per standard deviation).
- **Parity** (Table 4b). A straight line through the gap against effective
  studies reaches zero at 54 studies, 95% range 21 to unbounded, and 15% of
  bootstrap resamples never reach it. A curve with diminishing returns puts it in
  the hundreds or beyond. **Parity cannot be predicted from data spanning 1.6 to
  6.9 effective studies.** The "~20-25 studies" figure from the 2026-09-25 PI
  meeting came from the straight line on the old labels (23 [14, 94]) and should
  not be quoted.

**[revised]** The 2026-09-25 version said "diversity, not volume, is the binding
constraint" (study mix p = 0.014). That rested on the old BrC-T2D labels; moving
one cohort's labels moved p to 0.19. The diversity effect was never large and is
not robust.

What survives is descriptive and useful on its own: **the corpus has an
effective study count of 4.09, not 12.** One study holds 57% of the training
spectra and seven hold 1-76 each. Balanced subsampling can raise effective
diversity above the full corpus (`fixed2000_k12` reaches 6.88 on a quarter of the
rows), which is why "more data" and "more diverse data" are different requests.

### 3. Synthetic data does not help, even when it is realistic and diverse

Tables 6, 7 and 8.

**The realism test was corrected on 2026-10-26** (`code/analysis/realism_stratified.py`).
The original test grouped its folds by study. That flatters generators far from
the data and breaks for close ones: synthetic copies of a study sit in the
training folds labelled "synthetic" while the study's real spectra are tested,
so the AUC falls *below* 0.5 (0.24 for the diffusion model, 0.12 for autoencoder
reconstructions). With stratified folds, 0.5 again means indistinguishable. All
realism numbers below are from the fair test; the grouped values are kept in
Table 6 for traceability.

| generator | realism AUC (fair) | effective studies |
|---|---|---|
| GISSMO + lipids | 1.000 | — |
| GAN v1 / v2 / v2 study-mixing | 0.998 / 0.992 / 0.994 | 4.71 / 4.38 / 8.38 |
| VAE, prior samples | 1.000 | — |
| **Latent diffusion, corpus study mix** | **0.803** | 4.34 |
| **Latent diffusion, even study mix** | 0.878 | **9.79** |

- **GISSMO** (94-component basis, NNLS): median R2 0.25; three quarters of the
  signal is unmodelled.
- **GANs** (1D WGAN-GP). Detectable almost perfectly on the fair test. The
  2026-10-13 statement that GAN v2 reached 0.832 and that "the spectrum ends give
  it away" came from the grouped test: on the fair test, blanking the ends only
  moves 0.992 to 0.986, so the GAN is detectable throughout. The per-region and
  PCA findings (dense-centre coverage, 81% off-manifold variation) stand as
  descriptions, but the edges are not the main giveaway.
- **VAE** (KL-regularised 1D conv autoencoder, beta 0.01): prior samples are
  detectable perfectly; the prior does not match the encoded data.
- **Latent diffusion** (`code/generative/ldm_v2.py`): KL autoencoder (8.5M
  parameters, reflect padding, asinh intensity compression, L1 + first-difference
  loss; 32,768 points to 16 x 512) and a diffusion transformer on the latents
  (32.6M parameters, adaLN-Zero, v-prediction, Min-SNR-5, EMA, classifier-free
  guidance on study, DDIM). The most realistic generator by a wide margin (0.803),
  and with an even study mix the first synthetic set that more than doubles the
  corpus's effective study count (9.79) while staying reasonably realistic (0.878).
  **No memorisation:** a sample's closest training spectrum is as close as a real
  training spectrum's nearest other (median correlation 0.9969 vs 0.9971; share
  above 0.999: 4.6% vs 7.0%).
- **The direct tests.** Pretraining with 4,000 GAN v2 spectra: better 3, worse 10,
  level 8 (mean -0.016, Table 7). Pretraining with 1k / 4k / 16k even-mix
  diffusion spectra against the real-only baseline (16-region pooling, Table 8):
  mean -0.005 / +0.002 / +0.002; at most 6 of 21 comparisons better, each by
  0.009-0.018, all at small budgets; still behind standard ML in 15-17 of 21.
  Augmented runs trained 2-3x longer than the baseline, which favours them.

So synthetic data fails G3 even in its best form: realistic, not copied, and
carrying 2.4x the corpus's study diversity, it leaves transfer unchanged. This
agrees with G2, where real study diversity (4.1 to 5.9) did not help either.

## Phase 6: model size helps a little, then stops

Table 5. A two-sided sweep around the 4.0M baseline, 3 seeds per size.

| parameters | k=10 | k=all | gap to standard ML at k=all |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.82 M | 0.592 | 0.665 | -0.082 |
| 2.38 M | 0.598 | 0.666 | -0.081 |
| 3.99 M (baseline) | 0.618 | 0.685 | -0.062 |
| **10.54 M** | **0.634** | **0.706** | **-0.041** |
| 23.52 M | 0.635 | 0.700 | -0.047 |
| **standard ML** | **0.713** | **0.747** | — |

Larger is better up to ~10M parameters and flat after. Smaller is worse, so the
encoder is not over-sized for the data. The best size is still behind on every
task.

## Data changes since the corpus rebuild

- **MTBLS326 excluded** (2026-08-22). Cases are samples 1-27 and controls
  101-130, acquired as separate blocks; metabolite-free regions classify them at
  0.726. No method can separate batch from disease in this design. Details in
  [PHASE0_data_state.md](PHASE0_data_state.md).
- **BrC-T2D relabelled** (2026-10-15). The corrected label file lists 78
  patients; 8 changed class, 34 unlisted patients were dropped, and 3 repeat
  scans were removed so each patient contributes one spectrum (a repeat and its
  original could fall on opposite sides of a split). n = 78 means BrC-T2D now uses
  LOOCV, and the 4-class task supports only 5 per group. Standard ML's scores rose
  (cancer 0.855 -> 0.930, diabetes 0.764 -> 0.840, 4-class 0.597 -> 0.662),
  consistent with cleaner labels. Every BrC-dependent result was rerun; the
  115-spectrum versions are archived in `results/archive_brc115/`.

## Limitations, stated plainly

1. **Corpus diversity.** Effective 4.09 studies. Scaling conclusions are bounded
   by an axis this corpus can only traverse from 1.57 to 6.90.
2. **Cohort size.** 37-113 patients, by design. Full-data LOOCV intervals on
   Barth and BrC-T2D run 0.06 to 0.17 either side of the estimate, so several "level" verdicts are
   "cannot tell", not "equal". The measured single-run noise floor is 0.045.
3. **CPMG blood spectra only**, by upstream selection.
4. **One architecture family.** A patch transformer encoder; patch size, pooling,
   width and depth were swept, other families were not.
5. **Readout.** Every result except Table 2's 16-region column used mean pooling.
   Those are like-for-like comparisons, but their absolute gaps are probably about
   0.02 too pessimistic.
6. **G3 tested one basis and one GAN family.** A better generator could plausibly
   pass validation; the edge and TSP-area artefacts suggest where to start.
7. **Absolute referencing spread.** About 0.05 ppm between studies remains after
   the rebuild; judged natural variation, not corrected.

## What would change the answer

Stated as recommendations, since this is the useful output of a negative result:

- **More independent studies.** Not because the data show diversity pays (that
  effect did not hold up), but because it is the one axis this corpus could not
  reach: 4.1 effective studies is too few to test it.
- **Larger labelled cohorts**, in the hundreds, where adaptation has enough
  signal not to overfit and intervals are narrow enough to separate small effects.
- **For synthetic data**: a generator without border artefacts (padding is the
  first suspect) and with stable study conditioning, or a metabolite basis that
  covers the serum background.
- **Position-keeping pooling as the default readout** in any follow-up.

## Methodological notes worth carrying into the write-up

Each of these produced *plausible* rather than obviously broken results. They
are worth a methods paragraph, because each would have changed a conclusion:

1. **The axis defect.** Per-spectrum re-referencing plus resampling onto each
   spectrum's own ppm range. Every spectrum looked normal alone.
2. **Selection bias from best-of-N checkpoints.** An apparent Barth "SSL win" was
   the best of 15 checkpoints. Retracted twice before the summariser reported
   seed means.
3. **A crippled control.** The first fine-tuning design trained its own linear
   head, which underperformed the probe by 0.25. Fixed by giving every arm an
   identical readout.
4. **Batch confounding that a permutation test cannot see** (MTBLS326).
5. **Silent numeric failures.** `np.interp` on a descending axis returned zeros,
   so every GISSMO spectrum was noise; split seeds from Python's per-process
   `hash()` made "identical" splits differ; a results join on the wrong key
   reported "0 points" instead of failing; a shared filename pattern would have
   mixed 4M and 23M checkpoints.
6. **Readout defaults.** Mean pooling was inherited as a default after a July
   experiment had shown it costs accuracy; found and checked on 2026-10-23.
7. **Label provenance.** A cohort's labels changed after the main analysis and
   moved one headline p-value from 0.014 to 0.19.

The general lesson: on this project, wrong code and fragile data produced
confident, publishable-looking numbers rather than errors. Every headline number
should be reproducible from a committed result file, and every effect should be
checked for how much one cohort moves it.
